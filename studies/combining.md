# Combining the two studies

The TikTok study (`studies/tiktok/`) and the pixel-art study (`studies/pixel-art/`) are independent: separate
sources, code, notes, findings and skills (`tiktok-edit`, `pixel-art`). This page is the only place they meet:
what each says about a talking-head reel with a pixel mascot, and where they agree.

## Where they agree
- **Fewer, held moves beat constant motion.**
  - TikTok: the top quarter's face-led reels have about 3.5 graphic events a minute, and "heavy" is a faceless
    style.
  - Pixel art: one moving thing per beat and one big effect per reel. Poses hold 80-100 ms per drawing and idles
    are long and quiet.
  - Both answer Robert's "animations are overused".
- **The mascot is a performer with exits and returns.**
  - TikTok: 14 of 22 characters come back, and the mascots that appear once are flagged as a missed chance.
  - Pixel art: exits as events, returns with one change each time, and a held empty beat in between.
- **Change the picture, but one thing at a time.**
  - TikTok: change something every 10-15 s, since "too static" is the most common weakness.
  - Pixel art: one moving thing per beat. Clutter is layers competing.

## How they fit together in a reel (about 50 s)
| Time | TikTok rule | Pixel-art move for Fred |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Hook at 0.0 s; result or object by 2 s | Fred enters with a real move (crouch, launch, squash) acting out the hook verb, then exits as an event |
| 2-20 s | One graphic per idea; picture changes every 10-15 s | Fred is small in the background doing one task with 1-2 px moves while widgets carry the points |
| Middle | Proof within 3 s of each claim; list counter | Fred returns big and close to point at or tap the proof, holding the number; hold, snap, hold on the key word |
| Running gag | One idea, repeated in threes | Same setup three times, each reaction worse, the third breaks the pattern |
| 2-7 s before end | One comment-keyword CTA | Fred returns for the ask: his home shot with one visible change |
| End | Cut on the payoff, loop to the start | Empty frame held a beat, with the evidence of the gag left behind |

## Where they pull apart
- **Holding vs changing.** TikTok wants the picture to change every 10-15 s; pixel art wants long held poses.
  The two fit: Fred holds, and the change comes from Fred entering or leaving, a widget or a cut.
- **Text and sprites share the frame.** Captions sit in one fixed band inside the safe core (TikTok), and Fred
  never covers it (both).
