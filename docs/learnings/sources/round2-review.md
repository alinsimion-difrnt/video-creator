## 1. n6 Inflate: 45/60

**Scores.**
- Hook **6**: a huge soft pill reads "Best laptop for design?" at 0.3 s. It is clean and bold, but it is a speech bubble, not a surprise.
- Clarity **9**: the clearest of the six. A CMO would get Acme from the frames alone.
- Brand **8**: a true white studio with real cast shadows. Orange appears only as "you". Not navy, not paper.
- Motion **7**: the pills grow as you type, which is lovely, but some transitions run thin.
- Craft **7**: the matte silicone material is premium and the text is crisp DOM. The scanner beat at 23 s drops back to flat bubbles.
- Scales **8**: one material system can carry every beat of the 105 s film (prompts, answers, sources, reports, end card).

**Best three.**
1. **16.6–18.4 s: a deflated orange skin lies flat on the floor beside the inflated rival pills, labelled "Your brand?"** This is the best metaphor in the round: invisible means flat, recommended means inflated. You get it without reading.
2. **4.0–9.3 s: each question balloon grows with every keystroke.** By 8.5 s, three stacked bubbles fill the frame at about 60 px. This answers the brief's "every line has its image" perfectly.
3. **27.5–30.9 s: "Day 1 → Day 30" over a row of spheres that breathe**, with Acme's orange sphere growing from 11% to 24% and staying #2. It is the most readable "Every day" in the round, at about 48 px.

**Worst three.**
1. **19.75 s and 27.25 s: near-empty frames.** The whip to the logo leaves only a sliver of "rand?" (19.75), and the engines-to-rivals hand-off shows ghost skins and blank white (27.25). The skin itself reads as a thin sliver at 16.6–17.0.
2. **21.5–24.0 s, the "tracks" beat, goes flat.** Bubble rows with navy highlight boxes, a scanner line and "+1" pills look like UI, not the soft-3D world.
3. **The logo (20.3 s) and the "glossy" short list (15 s) don't sell the material.** The logo bevel looks chiseled rather than inflated, and the clearcoat list looks the same as the matte pills. Northwind is also clipped at the left edge at the end (30.3–30.9).

**Would the client like it? Yes.**
- It is white and orange, futuristic-soft, and has the chat bubbles he liked.
- Nothing in it looks like a report until 23 s, and nothing resembles a city.
- The risk is "toy". Keep the material matte and engineered, not candy.

**Hard rules.**
- Empty states: ✗ (19.75, 27.25).
- Logo on "Ack-me": ✓, complete by 20.24. It is motion-blurred at 20.0 and sharp at 20.25.
- "tracks…" has an image: ✓ (a scanner over real answer text, with brands counted).
- "Every day" readable: ✓, the best of the six.
- Text sizes: ✓, the largest of the six.
- Acme not #1: ✓ (#2).

### Recurring failures
1. **Whip and transition frames go empty.** Every film has 0.3–1.5 s of near-white frames when the camera travels between zones (n1 3.0 / 10.5 / 24.25; n3 5.25 / 24.5; n6 19.75 / 27.25; n2 24.25). Carrier objects must stay big through the move.
2. **White-on-white goes milky.** Without one strong dark value step, n2, n3 and n4 lose their structure at thumbnail size. Navy text is the only darkness, so the 3D needs contact shadows and one deep material.
3. **Orange drifts to salmon or red** under tone mapping (n4's key, early n3, n6's skin). The fix, darkening the albedo in linear space, is known; apply it everywhere.
4. **The last 4–7 s regress to report UI** (n1 day cards, n4 and n5 bar charts, n3's name columns). The client explicitly rejected this.
5. **Weak hooks.** Five of six open on "many small questions". Only n2's iris is a single striking image at 0.3 s.
6. **Small "Every day" dates** (n2, n5) and **cropped names** (n4, n6 at the end).

### Synthesis of the six LEARNINGS.md files
- **Text: keep every word as DOM over WebGL.** All six converged on this. Two variants work:
  - pin HTML to 3D faces with a per-frame homography (`matrix3d`, transform-origin 0 0), as in n1 and n4;
  - or use a CSS3D / WebGL / CSS3D sandwich sharing one camera, with `ShadowMaterial` casting onto HTML (n2, n3).
  Lay labels out large and scale them down. HTML occlusion needs clip-path hulls (n1). DOF on HTML means dividing the blur by the screen scale (n1, n5).
- **Camera:** use monotone (Fritsch–Carlson) splines. Catmull-Rom overshoots on whip-to-hold keys (n1, n3, n5). For zooms, keep the camera in log space, relative to the deepest level (n5). Give focus its own track (n4).
- **Carriers:** objects that cross a whip must be driven by the camera's progress, or have camera keys on their own path. Otherwise the frame goes 60% empty (n1, n6). This is the root cause of recurring failure 1.
- **Colour:**
  - three.js `Color(hex)` is already linear, so don't convert twice (n3);
  - NeutralToneMapping desaturates lit orange, so darken the albedo to about ×0.62 (n6);
  - use white #FFFFFF at exposure 1.2 to land around #F4F6F8 (n1);
  - key light from behind makes white type read on a white floor (n3);
  - white-on-white needs value steps from geometry, not paint (n2).
- **Materials:**
  - inflation is a superellipse profile driven by thickness, not a scale (n6);
  - glass needs structure behind it, or it reads as acrylic (n1);
  - a dot-matrix mask makes a light screen read as hardware (n4).
- **Cost on the shared 4-core box:**
  - three.js finals took 1.5–2 h; drafts 20–55 min;
  - pure DOM (n5) rendered in about 17 min, roughly 6× cheaper;
  - puppeteer stills via `__apply(t)` were everyone's real iteration loop;
  - run `check` on a 0.25-scale, no-blur build (n3).
- **Fonts:** load them before measuring or drawing canvas text (n2, n4, n6), and decompose composite glyphs for extruded type (n3).

**Develop n6 Inflate**, the soft, matte, white "pillow" world with orange as the only colour, **paired with n5's letter-dive** as the one-take transition grammar.
- Questions inflate as they are typed.
- The camera dives through a letter of the final question into ChatGPT's answer (n5, 9.4–11.4).
- Answer pills deflate. Brands puff up into a 2×2.

### Ten improvements for the current 105 s film (cut 2, `film/sfx/film-sfx.mp4`)
Cut 2 is competent light UI with good word timing and engine logos. Against the best of the six it is **flat, small and mostly empty white**:
- body text is often 16–24 px;
- many frames are more than 50% empty (5–11 s, 19–20 s, 45.0–46.75 s, 1:14–1:16);
- 22–44 s and 1:26–1:36 read as product report screens, which is exactly what he called "elements from our report";
- 1:37.5–1:45 is a 7 s static end card.

Ranked by lift per unit of cost:

1. **Scale every hero object up to at least 40% of the frame** (cheap: CSS transform). The single prompt bar at 5–11 s is about 30% of the width in a white void. Push in until it fills the frame, as n6 does at 8.5 s (60 px type).
2. **Make the question bar grow with each keystroke** (n6, 4–9 s) and stack the three questions as they come, instead of swapping them in place in one small bar (5.5–9.5 s). The work is DOM only.
3. **Replace the 9.5–11.25 s "question floats in empty space" with n5's dive through the "o" of "should" into the ChatGPT answer.** It is pure DOM (n5 rendered in about 17 min), and it removes the slowest stretch the client complained about ("8 to 20 is slow").
4. **Turn the 18.5–20.0 s tiny "5 [ ]" slot into a big orange element** that fills the frame: the deflated skin (n6) or the orange ring (n5). Then let it *become* the logo, as both do. Our current rounded-rect morph happens at about 15% of the frame width.
5. **Re-stage 22–30 s ("tracks… Every day") without the dashboard.** Use n5's multi-engine answer feed with boxed brands and an "N brands found" counter for "tracks", then one big readable time-lapse: n6's "Day 1 → Day 30" over breathing orbs, or n4's odometer. Drop the % cards and the Competitor breakdown table.
6. **Kill every empty transition frame** (45.0–46.75 white cards, 1:14–1:16 dot field, 1:36.75 blank). Drive the carriers from camera progress (n1 / n6 learnings), so the outgoing object stays large until the incoming one lands.
7. **Add one depth layer everywhere** (cheap: CSS shadows plus DOF blur on the background plane). Give cards real contact shadows and a value step (#FFFFFF card on a #EEF1F5 floor) so the light UI stops reading as flat paper. n1, n6 and n3 all prove that white needs a shadow to read as "premium".
8. **Make the 30–45 s "how it works" steps physical instead of screen-recorded.** For example, the typed prompt inflates and flies into its row as a pill, and the "Suggested for you" chips puff in. Keep the step numeral, but at three times the size.
9. **Animate the end card** (1:37.5–1:45, 7 s static). Keep the camera pushing slowly, inflate the logo in once, and resolve "Free plan · No card needed · acme.example" on beats. Today it breaks his "no static >2–3 s" rule.
10. **Give orange one meaning and a material.** Today orange is used for buttons, outlines, send arrows and Acme alike. Reserve solid #00AA90 for "your brand / Acme" only, as a physical object (skin, ring or plinth rim), and render the send button in navy. Every top-3 direction gets its clarity from this rule.

The cheapest big wins are 1, 2, 4, 6 and 9: CSS-only changes in the existing segments, with no WebGL.
The biggest single lift is 3 plus 5, adopting n5's dive and feed in segments A and B, which pure DOM keeps within budget.
