## 2. Visual trends for AI brands (2025 and 2026)

Already cliché (avoid, or use only ironically):
- Purple, blue and pink gradients, starbursts, sparkles, "the AI glow." Critics call this the "AI purple problem":
  https://www.indiehackers.com/post/the-ai-purple-problem-why-every-ai-brand-looks-the-same-6cb0aa2a02
- Circular, pulsating, radial "orb" or tunnel logos. Critics cite Gemini, Copilot, Krea and Ideogram:
  https://velvetshark.com/ai-company-logos-that-look-like-buttholes
- Neural nets, particle morphs, holograms, dark HUDs, glowing brains, and Matrix-style code. These overlap
  with ideas we already tried.
- Generic "glass card" UI floating in space. Since Apple's Liquid Glass shipped, this is OS default, not brand.

Still fresh, if used with discipline:
- **AI as a physical instrument.** Teenage Engineering and Nothing: tactile hardware, dials, LEDs, white
  housings, and one hot accent colour. Orange belongs here.
- **Soft-body, inflatable and squishy 3D, plus tactile matte materials.** Trend roundups for 2026 report
  "puffy, inflated 3D" and "squishy type" everywhere:
  https://www.krumzi.com/blog/12-graphic-design-trends-shaping-2026-and-how-ai-is-changing-the-game
  It is fresh for B2B or AI, but becoming common in consumer work. Keep it matte and precise, not candy-coloured.
- **Kinetic and generative typography** as the main actor: variable-weight type, displacement shaders,
  text that reacts to sound. This fits a product about which names get said.
- **Light as an edge or perimeter event,** as in Siri's edge glow, rather than an orb.
- **Low-resolution dot matrix on white,** in the style of the Nothing Glyph Matrix. It is honest and
  hardware-like, and it suits orange LEDs.
- **Real physics in white space,** as in Lusion and Igloo: weight, balance, collisions, caustics.
  It reads as "engineered," not sci-fi.
- **Restraint, as in OpenAI's site and Anthropic's film.** Near-white canvas, one accent colour, the product carries meaning.

Rule of thumb for Acme: put the frame on white, give it one physical metaphor that has weight, make
type the hero, and use orange only for "you." The future should come from precision and physics, not from glow.

## 3. Twelve concept seeds for the first 30 s

Shared beats: B1 people ask AI everything → B2 three typed questions → B3 ChatGPT answers with 4–5 names →
B4 "Is yours one of them?" with an empty orange fifth slot → B5 Acme logo → B6 tracks which brands AI
recommends in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity → B7 every day, against the competitors you pick.
None of these use a city or any of the ten ideas already tried. Every seed keeps the camera moving, with
no hold longer than 2–3 s.

### 1. The Shortlist Mobile
A Calder-style hanging mobile in an infinite white studio, made of matte white arms and soft-shadowed discs.
Every question adds weight. The structure tips, swings and settles into a balance where only a few
discs hang at the lowest, most visible level.
- **B1–B2:** Typed questions drop in as small weights on thin wires, one after another. The camera orbits slowly.
- **B3:** The mobile settles, and four discs swing forward with brand names in kinetic type.
- **B4:** A fifth hook swings into frame, empty, with an orange disc-shaped outline casting an orange shadow.
- **B5:** The orange disc clicks onto the hook and the whole mobile rebalances around it. The logo is the counterweight.
- **B6:** Three main arms are labelled ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.
- **B7:** A day counter ticks and the balance shifts slightly each day. Competitor discs are rimmed in grey.
- **Ownable:** "Visibility is a balance you can shift," and balance reads as physics.
- **Risk:** The physics simulation has to look believable, and the motion can feel slow. Keep the swings snappy.

### 2. The Answer Sorter (marble run)
A white precision marble run, closer to a lab instrument than a toy. Questions are glossy white spheres
that run through gates, chutes and switches that decide where they land.
- **B1–B2:** Three spheres, each with a typed question etched on it, drop in. The camera follows one down the track in a single take.
- **B3:** Spheres land in a row of cups, and four cups light up with brand names.
- **B4:** The fifth cup is orange and empty, and a sphere rattles past it.
- **B5:** The Acme badge sits on the machine's housing, and the camera pulls back to show it.
- **B6:** The track splits into three lanes for ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.
- **B7:** A conveyor keeps feeding spheres in a time-lapse. A tally per cup compares you with the competitors you picked.
- **Ownable:** It turns Acme into "see how the machine decides," and the routing reads as mechanism.
- **Risk:** It could read as a toy or a Rube Goldberg machine. Keep it clean and industrial (anodised white, orange only for you).

### 3. The Instrument
A Teenage Engineering-style white device in macro close-up. It has a dial, a dot screen, three physical
keys and an orange key.
- **B1–B2:** A finger types the three questions on mechanical keys, and the dot screen scrolls them.
- **B3:** A thermal-style strip slides out with four names. It is a glossy synthetic strip, not paper.
- **B4:** The slot for line 5 blinks orange and empty.
- **B5:** The orange key is pressed, and the Acme logo is engraved on the faceplate.
- **B6:** A three-position selector switches between ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, and the screen redraws for each.
- **B7:** A date wheel rolls, and a competitor bar rises and falls on the dot screen.
- **Ownable:** It makes Acme a precise measuring device, and the orange key is literally "your brand."
- **Risk:** It is close to Rabbit and Teenage Engineering territory, and it needs good product rendering. Tie the device design to the logo.

### 4. Type Tide
A white field covered with thousands of tiny brand names in light grey type, like a sea of names.
- **B1–B2:** Each typed question sends a ripple across the field, and names surge toward it.
- **B3:** Four names rise out of the field as large, heavy, extruded type. The rest sink.
- **B4:** A fifth position rises empty: an orange outline of a missing word. The camera finds a tiny orange name still deep in the field.
- **B5:** The orange name flows up into the slot and resolves into the Acme logo.
- **B6:** The tide splits into three currents labelled ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.
- **B7:** The water level rises and falls day by day, and competitor names are tagged.
- **Ownable:** It makes the product's core question visible as type: which names surface.
- **Risk:** Text this dense can look busy. Keep the background names at about 10% contrast and the type crisp.

### 5. Inflate
Chat bubbles as matte soft-body balloons (pearl-white silicone) in a white space.
- **B1–B2:** Questions type inside bubbles that inflate with each keystroke and bump into one another.
- **B3:** An answer bubble splits into four inflated name pills.
- **B4:** A fifth pill is a flat, deflated orange skin.
- **B5:** It inflates with a soft squeak and becomes the Acme logo.
- **B6:** Three bubble streams carry the engine labels.
- **B7:** Each day the pills inflate or deflate with share of voice, measured against competitor pills.
- **Ownable:** Keeps the chat bubbles the client loved, and gives them physical meaning: inflated means recommended.
- **Risk:** The 2026 "puffy" trend could look like candy. Use matte materials, precise edges and no rainbow colours.

### 6. Caret One-Take
The text caret is the protagonist. A single camera move follows a blinking vertical line through white
space, with no UI chrome.
- **B1:** The caret moves through drifting fragments of questions people ask.
- **B2:** It types three questions in large type, and each one slides away in perspective.
- **B3:** It streams an answer of four names, written live.
- **B4:** At line 5 it stops and blinks, and turns orange.
- **B5:** The orange caret extends into the Acme logo stroke.
- **B6:** Three carets type in parallel for ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity.
- **B7:** A calendar of carets, one per day, with competitor lines in grey.
- **Ownable:** Minimal, product-true and cheap to build, and the orange caret becomes a brand asset.
- **Risk:** It can tip into the "report UI" look the client rejected. It needs kinetic, physical type and constant camera motion.

### 7. Aperture
A large white mechanical iris, shown as camera aperture blades.
- **B1–B2:** The aperture is wide open, showing a blur of everything. Each question closes it one stop.
- **B3:** Through the narrow opening, four names are in sharp focus.
- **B4:** A fifth position at the edge of the aperture is cut off and glows orange.
- **B5:** The blades are machined into the Acme logo shape.
- **B6:** Three apertures, one for each engine.
- **B7:** The aperture opens and closes daily, with competitors inside or outside the circle.
- **Ownable:** "AI narrows the whole web to five names" as a single mechanical gesture.
- **Risk:** It is circular, so it may read as an orb or radar. Keep it mechanical, with blades and metal, and never glowing.

### 8. Caustic Table
Sunlight through moving water projects caustic patterns onto a white surface.
- **B1–B2:** Typed questions disturb the water, and the caustics scatter.
- **B3:** The light concentrates into four bright points, each forming a name.
- **B4:** A fifth point is dim, with an orange tint.
- **B5:** The orange light focuses and resolves into the logo.
- **B6:** Three water basins, one per engine.
- **B7:** Light patterns shift through the day like a sundial, with competitor points tracked.
- **Ownable:** Being recommended is shown as "light lands on you."
- **Risk:** Rendering caustics is expensive, and the look could drift into wellness branding. Keep it sharp and technical.

### 9. Lens Stack
Thick Liquid Glass discs slide over very large typography.
- **B1–B2:** Each question is magnified as a lens passes over it.
- **B3:** Four lenses lock over four names.
- **B4:** A fifth lens sits over an empty orange word slot.
- **B5:** The lens refracts the slot into the logo.
- **B6:** The three lenses are tinted to separate the engines.
- **B7:** Lenses track daily movement, with competitor names visible under lenses.
- **Ownable:** "We bring your brand into focus in AI."
- **Risk:** It is close to the tried glass prism and to Apple's default look. Treat it as a fallback or as a single beat inside another concept.

### 10. Glyph Matrix
A white dot-matrix wall, with dots as physical pins or LEDs.
- **B1–B2:** Questions scroll across in dots.
- **B3:** Four names rendered in dots.
- **B4:** A fifth row of unlit dots, with an orange outline.
- **B5:** The dots light orange, and the Acme logo forms.
- **B6:** Three walls, one per engine.
- **B7:** Daily rows like a contribution graph, with competitor rows.
- **Ownable:** Honest, data-like and hardware-native. Orange LEDs are distinctive.
- **Risk:** It is close to Nothing's identity. It needs our own grid size and shape, such as Acme's logo geometry.

### 11. Droste Answer
An infinite zoom (the Droste effect) through white space, using zoom instead of a flight.
- **B1–B2:** The camera zooms into a question, and the next question sits inside a letter of it.
- **B3:** The zoom lands on the answer list of four names.
- **B4:** The camera zooms into the empty orange fifth line.
- **B5:** Inside the line sits the Acme logo.
- **B6:** Three nested layers, one per engine.
- **B7:** A zoom-out reveals daily tiles with competitors.
- **Ownable:** It delivers the one-take feel without a camera flight, so it doesn't repeat what we tried.
- **Risk:** It can cause motion sickness, and repeated zooms get monotonous. Vary the speed curves.

### 12. Pearl Ferrofluid
A pearl-white magnetic fluid on a white surface.
- **B1–B2:** Each typed question acts as a magnetic field, and the fluid spikes toward it.
- **B3:** The fluid forms four peaks, each carrying a name.
- **B4:** A fifth magnet slot is orange and empty, and the fluid hesitates.
- **B5:** The orange magnet pulls the fluid into the Acme logo.
- **B6:** Three magnets, one per engine.
- **B7:** Peaks grow and fall daily against competitor peaks.
- **Ownable:** "AI as a material," and orange as the attractor that pulls the answer toward you.
- **Risk:** Ferrofluid is usually black, so the white version is unproven. The simulation is hard to render.

## 4. Recommended shortlist to prototype first

1 (Mobile), 2 (Sorter), 4 (Type Tide) and 5 (Inflate). These are physical, white, type-led and not cliché.
6 (Caret) is the cheap fallback.
